# 2. Planning and tracking

What gets done in which order, what was decided along the way, and what was learned.

1. **One ordered backlog.** Work is picked up from the top; the order is the owner's.
2. **Backlog items are atomic and as small as possible:** each can be finished, reviewed
   and closed on its own.
3. **A check-in with the owner every three to five finished items:** a quick confirmation
   or a reordering.
4. **Three records, each with one job:** the backlog (what is still to do), a status
   record (what is done; a finished item moves there), and a decision log (the questions
   asked and the answers given).
5. **No dates or names on rules and backlog items;** the history is in version control.
   The one exception: a measured number keeps the date it was measured on, next to it.
6. **A backlog item refers to the rules that apply to it instead of repeating them:** an
   item "review the database code" points to the review rules.
7. **Planning notes that contain personal information stay in a private place,** apart
   from anything published.
8. **Every plan ends with a retrospective.**
   - Review all interactions of the project; find the recurring patterns and themes in
     the feedback, questions and corrections.
   - Turn each into a rule in the right section of this way of working, or sharpen an
     existing one.
   - The owner approves every change to the rules.
